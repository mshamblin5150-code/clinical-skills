## Conflicts

No unresolved same-population, same-quantity conflict was identified. The prevention
recommendation applies only to people at increased risk who do not currently have a
depression diagnosis. Current depression, high symptom levels, or active treatment belong
to a treatment pathway rather than this preventive evidence review.

The related USPSTF depression-screening recommendations and external ACOG postpartum
screening action address detection, not preventive counseling eligibility. They are retained
with distinct quantities and provenance and do not replace the recommendation to provide
or refer increased-risk pregnant and postpartum people for counseling.

The counseling schedules are evidence ranges or named-program examples, not a single
required regimen. Medication and omega-3 findings are small-trial evidence and harms
context; the source does not recommend these as substitutes for counseling. The source
states that evidence for noncounseling prevention is limited or mixed.

The `minimum of 2 weeks` depressed-mood or loss-of-interest boundary belongs to the
source's depressive-disorder definition, whereas baby blues usually resolves within
`10 days` after delivery. Neither duration is a preventive-counseling eligibility threshold;
the prevention recommendation instead requires increased risk without a current depression
diagnosis.
