## Benefits, harms, uncertainty, and implementation boundaries

- Treat sepsis and septic shock as medical emergencies. Obtain appropriate cultures promptly when this does not materially delay antimicrobials, reassess infection probability and antimicrobial spectrum/duration daily, and pursue timely source control. Balance delay harm against diagnostic yield and procedure risk.
- Reassess perfusion dynamically rather than continuing fluid solely to a fixed cumulative volume. Account for fluid overload, pulmonary edema, abdominal compartment effects, kidney injury, cardiac dysfunction, and setting-specific access to intensive monitoring; use age-specific fluid type, bolus, and stopping rules.
- Use age-specific vasoactive pathways. Titrate to perfusion and blood-pressure targets, monitor ischemia and arrhythmia, and escalate/add agents according to shock phenotype and response rather than transferring adult drug order or dose assumptions to children.
- Apply lung-protective ventilation, prone positioning, neuromuscular blockade, recruitment/PEEP, oxygen, and extracorporeal-support recommendations only to the defined hypoxemia/ARDS population. Balance ventilator injury, barotrauma, hemodynamic compromise, sedation, weakness, and resource requirements.
- Steroid recommendations differ by age, shock persistence, and exposure risk. Account for hyperglycemia, hypernatremia, neuromuscular weakness, infection, gastrointestinal bleeding, and uncertainty about agent, dose, and duration.
- Transfusion, renal replacement, glucose control, venous thromboembolism/stress-ulcer prophylaxis, nutrition, mobilization, sedation, analgesia, delirium care, and family/goals-of-care decisions remain conditional on the exact age-specific population and threshold in the cited row.
- Explicit uncertainty or absence of a recommendation is retained as such and is not converted into a recommendation. Weak/conditional guidance requires patient/family preferences, feasibility, equity, and resource context.

## Conflicts

Adult and pediatric values are maintained as different-source, age-specific branches rather than same-population conflicts. Apparent differences in initial fluid volume and bolus strategy, crystalloid choice, transfusion thresholds, blood-pressure goals, vasoactive sequence, ventilation, corticosteroids, glucose, nutrition, renal replacement, and follow-up resolve by age, shock phenotype, resource setting, comorbidity, and certainty. Where the two guidelines address the same age group and purpose differently, the row remains source-labeled and no harmonized value is invented.

The pediatric source has one unresolved within-source population discrepancy for current statement 39: its comparison table limits the conservative SpO2 target to intubated children after resuscitation, whereas the full current restatement applies it to all children after resuscitation. The `peds-current-20` row retains both printed populations and the shared 88-92% versus >94% action without silently selecting one population.
