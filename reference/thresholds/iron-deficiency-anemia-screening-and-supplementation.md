## Conflicts

No unresolved same-population, same-quantity conflict was identified. The two USPSTF I
statements separately conclude that evidence is insufficient for screening and for
routine supplementation; neither is a recommendation against the service. External
ACOG, American Academy of Family Physicians, and CDC screening schedules and the CDC
and ACOG supplementation actions remain explicitly attributed external guidance. Their
populations, methods, timing, and exceptions are not rewritten as USPSTF conclusions.

The p6 study definitions using ferritin `<12 or 20 mcg/L` and hemoglobin thresholds
from `<10.0` through `<12.1 g/dL` describe evidence populations and time points. They
do not conflict with the p4 conclusion that no consensus exact ferritin level indicates
iron deficiency, and they are not promoted to universal diagnostic thresholds.

Routine supplementation means low-dose iron or iron-fortified food without measuring
hematologic indices. It is distinct from iron therapy after an abnormal screen and from
ACOG treatment of diagnosed iron deficiency anemia. The CDC `30 mg/day` preventive
dose is therefore not treated as a dose for diagnosed anemia.

- retained both I statements as separate screening and routine-supplementation
  decisions, their asymptomatic pregnant adolescent and adult population, every stated
  exclusion, and the individualized clinical-judgment boundary;
- retained screening with hemoglobin or hematocrit proxies, the possible iron-therapy

  a consensus ferritin cutoff, and the low-dose-without-testing definition of routine
  supplementation;

  inadequate risk-tool evidence, limited screening-harm evidence, and gastrointestinal
  adverse effects without turning risk association into eligibility;
- retained the distinct p2 inadequate screening-benefit and screening-harm conclusions,
  the p5 absence of direct screening-plus-treatment benefit evidence, and the p6 absence
  of screening-harm trials; retained the separate p2 and p5 routine-supplementation
  benefit findings without treating them as conflicting conclusions;
- retained improved maternal hematologic indices, small-or-less supplementation harms,
  gastrointestinal harms for supplementation or treatment recipients, and the absence
  of infant-harm reporting without treating intermediate improvement as proof of
  maternal or infant health benefit;
- retained trial hemoglobin and ferritin ranges only as evidence-population definitions.
  Supplementation start times, outcome definitions such as birth weight `<2500 g` and
  `<10th percentile`, sample sizes, effect estimates, event rates, and adherence figures
  were trial design or outcome evidence and were not promoted to patient-action
  thresholds;
- retained external ACOG, American Academy of Family Physicians, CDC, and Dietary
  Guidelines actions with their exact timing, dose, populations, exclusions, evaluation,
  treatment, and diet-discussion boundaries;
- prevalence percentages, survey and public-comment dates, article metadata, author and
  disclosure information, citation numbers, and reference-list values were not
  interpreted as additional patient-action thresholds.
