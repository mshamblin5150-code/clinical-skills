## Conflicts

The routine diagnostic-serology prohibition and the postdiarrheal-HUS serology option are complementary populations, not a conflict: general serology does not establish acute etiology, while CDC-validated serogroup testing may recover evidence of antecedent STEC after culture and toxin testing fail.

The no-follow-up-testing default and jurisdiction-directed serial cultures are complementary management settings. The source's illustrative Typhi and STEC clearance schedules are explicitly examples of variable local rules, not universal federal thresholds.

Rows that share a broad clinical topic but govern different actions are intentionally keyed separately rather than treated as conflicts: diagnostic eligibility versus specimen count; public-health culture recovery versus susceptibility culture; patient versus contact empiric therapy; primary testing versus additional cultures versus serology avoidance; endoscopy versus duodenal aspiration; start versus narrow versus no-isolate enteric-fever therapy; routine follow-up versus return-setting culture and source-printed clearance examples; etiologic marker restrictions versus clinically useful blood counts; HUS laboratory monitoring versus smear escalation; initial, ongoing-loss, nasogastric, correction-endpoint, and IV rehydration branches; human milk versus usual-diet feeding; distinct organism-agent selections; hygiene, care, product, food, education, and activity-prevention actions; bacterial versus viral/protozoal specimens; the distinct STEC assay questions; and typhoid eligibility, booster, and external schedule details. These are complementary actions or populations, not competing values for one decision.

Table 6's organism-specific treatment branches are subordinate to recommendation 38's instruction to modify or discontinue treatment when a clinically plausible organism is identified. Its source-era C. difficile choices are related guidance pending the then-in-development IDSA/SHEA update and do not supersede that dedicated guideline.

The guideline's rotavirus, typhoid, and cholera recommendations incorporate external ACIP/CDC policy. The detailed Ty21a and Vi ages and booster intervals are source-printed external schedule details, not new IDSA-created schedules; the source's cholera recommendation is limited to the 2017 US live-vaccine availability and the stated 18-64-year population.
