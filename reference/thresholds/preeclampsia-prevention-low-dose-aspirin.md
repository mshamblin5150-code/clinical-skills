## Conflicts

CONFLICT: moderate-risk-aspirin-eligibility | pregnant-two-plus-moderate-risk | `low-dose aspirin may provide benefit when at least 2 moderate-risk factors are present, but evidence for this approach is less certain` versus `Table 1 also classifies combinations of multiple moderate-risk factors under high risk and says to recommend low-dose aspirin`; both source statements are preserved. The sheet does not silently promote every moderate-risk combination to the certainty of a single stated high-risk factor.

The USPSTF action begins after 12 weeks and supplies no separate stop week. The external
ACOG and Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine schedule of 12 to 28 weeks, optimally
before 16 weeks, continued until delivery is compatible but more specific and remains
explicitly external. The trial range of 60 to 150 mg daily is evidence, not a replacement
for the USPSTF's 81 mg daily action.

ADR 0009 disposition:

- retained the Grade B aspirin action, exact 81 mg daily dose, start after 12 weeks,
  high-risk pregnancy population, and prior-adverse-effect or contraindication boundary;
- retained each high-risk factor, the two-or-more moderate-risk rule and its source
  ambiguity, the low-risk do-not-recommend row, and the social rather than biological
  meaning of Black identity and lower income in risk assessment without treating either
  factor alone as automatic aspirin eligibility;
- retained history-based risk assessment, predictive-model accuracy limitations,
  the qualified at-least-8% absolute-risk context, clinical judgment, and shared
  benefit-harm discussion without inventing a biomarker, imaging cutoff, or validated
  risk score;
- retained the source's preeclampsia blood-pressure, gestational-age, proteinuria, and
  organ-finding definitions because they alter classification; prevalence, trial event
  rates, and pooled effect estimates were treated as evidence rather than new actions;
- separated the evaluated 60 to 150 mg dose range and the limitation that 81 mg was not
  directly studied from the recommended, reasonable US 81 mg dose;
- did not invent a USPSTF stop week: trial continuation until delivery or near term is
  labeled as an evidence boundary, while the explicit until-delivery action remains
  external ACOG and Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine guidance;
- retained substantial benefits, the small-harm assessment, uncommon bleeding,
  statistically nonsignificant bleeding outcomes, and limited longer-term child and
  subgroup harm evidence without converting absence of demonstrated harm into proof of
  no harm;
- kept the FDA NSAID warning and exception, the ACOG and Society for Maternal-Fetal
  Medicine regimen, and WHO and American Heart Association/American Stroke Association
  positions explicitly external; no external dose or schedule was invented where the
  source provides none;
- research questions, study sample sizes and demographics, effect estimates, public-
  comment and correction dates, article metadata, disclosures, citation numbers, and
  reference-list values were not interpreted as additional patient-action thresholds.
