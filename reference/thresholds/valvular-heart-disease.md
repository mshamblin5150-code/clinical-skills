## Scope

**Read:** all 156 source pages, including front matter, top messages, methods,
general principles, every disease and prosthetic-valve section, all recommendation
tables, staging and severity tables, algorithms, figures, supportive text,
special-population sections, future directions, organizational material, disclosures,
and references. The exact record contains 259 recommendations in 54 tables. Every
record recommendation is retained as a directly cited threshold row; the disease
staging and surveillance rows below add patient-changing decision points not indexed
as recommendation records.

**Not read:** nothing in the source page range. Reference pages were inspected for
scope and retired by class because they contain citations rather than clinical prose.

**Scoped out under ADR 0009:** study sizes, dates, prevalence, effect estimates,
confidence intervals, citation numerals, and research or historical numbers that do
not alter diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, intervention, prosthesis choice, or
follow-up. Qualitative recommendations remain because the exact record treats them
as formal actions and because many determine intervention, contraindication, or
shared-decision branches.

## Benefits, harms, implementation, and uncertainty

- Intervention is driven by symptoms, hemodynamic severity, ventricular response,
  pulmonary pressure, exercise findings, biomarkers, progression, anatomy, life
  expectancy, procedural risk, and patient goals. Discordant clinical and imaging
  findings require repeat or alternative testing rather than automatic intervention.
- Valve-center and multidisciplinary-team evaluation is central for severe disease,
  repair feasibility, transcatheter versus surgical choice, multiple comorbidities,
  pregnancy, endocarditis, and prosthetic dysfunction.
- SAVR, TAVI, repair, replacement, commissurotomy, transcatheter edge-to-edge repair,
  valve-in-valve procedures, and aortic surgery trade mortality, stroke, bleeding,
  vascular injury, pacemaker, paravalvular leak, reoperation, durability, anatomy,
  coronary access, and recovery. Age cutoffs are decision aids, not substitutes for
  life expectancy, anatomy, feasibility, and informed preference.
- Antithrombotic choices trade valve thrombosis and systemic embolism against maternal,
  fetal, and general bleeding. Mechanical valves require VKA-based regimens; NOAC
  prohibitions, INR targets, aspirin additions, bridging, reversal, and pregnancy
  anti-Xa monitoring remain population-specific.
- Pregnancy can destabilize severe stenosis or regurgitation. Warfarin embryopathy,
  fetal loss, maternal thrombosis, heparin failure, radiation, bypass, and prematurity
  require shared maternal-fetal decisions and exact peripartum timing.
- Endocarditis actions balance uncontrolled infection, HF, abscess, embolism, stroke,
  intracranial hemorrhage, operative mortality, relapse, and prosthesis infection.
  Obtain cultures before antibiotics when feasible and use infectious-disease and
  surgical expertise.
- Evidence is limited for several asymptomatic, right-sided, mixed-valve, pregnancy,
  transcatheter, and redo-procedure branches. COR 2b recommendations state weaker
  action force; source-table evidence levels are not encoded in this sheet and must
  not be inferred from the COR field.

## Conflicts and provenance

CONFLICT: echo-surveillance-frequency — Table 5 gives standard single-lesion intervals,
while multiple-valve disease, a dilating ventricle, new symptoms, pregnancy, or changing
examination findings require earlier testing. The shorter clinical interval controls.

Reconciliation — the indexed pregnancy-anticoagulation recommendations contain
alternative first-trimester strategies stratified by warfarin dose, patient preference,
and ability to perform strict anti-Xa monitoring. These are mutually exclusive shared-
decision branches, not a universal substitution schedule.

SOURCE-PRINTED EXTERNAL: antibiotic prophylaxis, antimicrobial regimens, pregnancy
anticoagulation, CAD revascularization, and noncardiac-surgery recommendations invoke
or adapt AHA, ACC, infectious-disease, obstetric, and anticoagulation standards. They
