## Conflicts

No same-population, same-quantity conflict was identified. The USPSTF I statement is
neither for nor against speech-and-language-specific screening and establishes no
interval. The external AAP schedule concerns broader developmental screening, which may
include speech and language domains but is not specific to this condition. The Canadian
Task Force position likewise concerns standardized developmental-delay screening, so
neither external position is rewritten as a USPSTF conflict.

The often-used value of at least 1 standard deviation below the mean does not conflict
with the statement that no universally accepted delay threshold exists: the source
expressly states both and qualifies the numeric value as what is often considered a
delay, not a universal diagnostic cutoff.

CONFLICT: race-ethnicity-prevalence-anomaly — `summary/comments: Black and Hispanic/Latino children have higher rates than White children` conflicts with the same-page printed `survey: non-Hispanic Black approximately 10%, Hispanic 6.9%, and White 7.8%`, because the Hispanic value is lower than the White value. This source-internal inconsistency is prevalence evidence, not a screening-eligibility rule. The separately described low-income/public-insurance disparity does not share this numeric contradiction.
