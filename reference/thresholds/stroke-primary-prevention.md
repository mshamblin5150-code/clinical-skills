## Benefits, harms, and implementation boundaries

- Primary prevention applies to people with no prior stroke. A row does not convert
  management of an established stroke, TIA, or another disease into primary
  prevention.
- AF anticoagulation is preference-sensitive and requires thromboembolic and bleeding
  risk assessment. The guideline's approximate 2% annual-risk boundary is not a
  substitute for the current AF guideline or individualized contraindication review.
- Potassium-enriched salt may provoke hyperkalemia in kidney disease or with potassium
  supplements or potassium-sparing drugs. Dietary supplements otherwise listed as no
  benefit should not displace dietary quality, activity, BP, lipid, or tobacco care.
- Bariatric surgery has procedural and nutritional harms; eligibility and choice
  require multidisciplinary assessment. CPAP evidence is stroke-outcome limited even
  when treatment is indicated for sleep-apnea symptoms or other outcomes.
- Intensive glycemic lowering to A1c at most 6.5% adds hypoglycemia burden without
  stroke-prevention benefit. Antihypertensive selection must account for pregnancy,
  kidney function, electrolytes, angioedema, orthostasis, and drug interactions.
- Asymptomatic-carotid population screening can generate false positives and
  downstream procedure harm. The over-70% revascularization branch presupposes a
  confirmed lesion, low perioperative risk, intensive medical therapy, sufficient
  life expectancy, multidisciplinary review, and shared decision-making; it does not
  justify routine screening.
- Sickle-cell TCD, transfusion, MRI, hydroxyurea, and transplant branches are pediatric
  hematology pathways. Chronic transfusion has alloimmunization and iron-overload
  burdens; hydroxyurea substitution requires normalized velocities and absence of
  intracranial stenosis.
- Aspirin, ticagrelor, warfarin, DOACs, and full-dose heparins have major bleeding and
  intracranial-bleeding harms. APS rows depend on persistent antibody profile and
  clinical phenotype; VKA is preferred after unprovoked venous thrombosis because
  DOAC trials showed more arterial thrombosis. Cancer-associated primary prevention
  remains unestablished.
- Severe pregnancy-associated hypertension is an emergency extending through 6 weeks
  postpartum. The below-160/110 acute target and below-140/90 HDP goal are different
  populations and purposes. Contraceptive, menopausal, gender-affirming, and
  testosterone decisions remain individualized; do not infer safety for unstudied
  formulations, off-label testosterone use, or populations outside the stated bounds.
- Tobacco pharmacotherapy selection accounts for contraindications, pregnancy, drug
  interactions, and prior response. E-cigarette long-term cerebrovascular benefit and
  relapse/dual-use consequences are uncertain.

## Definitions and evidence-only calibrators

- Primary prevention means prevention in an individual without a previous stroke.
  CSVD is radiographic white-matter hyperintensity, recent small subcortical infarct,
  lacune, microbleed, enlarged perivascular spaces, or cerebral atrophy; the SCI rows
  focus on subcortical silent infarcts and do not infer the same mechanism for silent
  cortical infarcts.
- Stage 1 hypertension is 130-139/80-89 mm Hg and stage 2 is at least 140 or at least
  90 mm Hg in the source framework. Higher ASCVD risk informs medication at stage 1.
- Class II obesity is BMI 35-39.9 kg/m2. High perioperative carotid risk includes
  estimated life expectancy 3-5 years, serious cardiac/pulmonary disease, or expected
  stroke/death risk over 3%.
- High-risk aPL requires persistent lupus anticoagulant, double/triple aPL positivity,
  or persistently high titers; persistence is at least 2 occasions at least 12 weeks
  apart. Low risk is isolated low-to-medium anticardiolipin or anti-beta2-glycoprotein
  1, particularly when transient.
- **EVIDENCE ONLY:** sedentary stroke risk in cohort evidence began around 3.7 h/day;
  above 6.5 h/day risk rose about 6% per additional hour and above 11 h/day about 21%
  per hour. These are associations, not prescribed sitting limits.
- **EVIDENCE ONLY:** carotid auscultation sensitivity was 56% and positive predictive
  value 25%; severe stenosis carried about 15% ipsilateral stroke risk over 5 years in
  pooled historical evidence. These values do not establish screening eligibility.
- **EVIDENCE ONLY:** ticagrelor plus aspirin reduced stroke modestly in PEGASUS but
  major bleeding was 2.6% with 60 mg versus 1.06% with placebo. Aspirin in ASPREE
  added 0.7% absolute intracranial bleeding without primary-stroke benefit.

## Blinds and limitations

- The PDF text layer was the primary cold-read surface. Tables and layouts were read
  in page context when adjacency changed population, class, or threshold meaning.
- Recommendation markers are bound only where the exact JSON supplies a `rec_id`.
  Ten source-printed recommendation-table actions are retained with narrative
  locators because the index omits them; their absence from the index is not evidence
  that the actions are absent from the guideline.
- Knowledge gaps establish uncertainty, not a negative recommendation. Observational
  risk estimates and trial event rates are labeled evidence-only and do not become
  treatment thresholds.
- No inference is made beyond the stated age, pregnancy/postpartum, disease,
  stenosis, antibody-profile, or bleeding-risk populations. Drug doses printed only
  as trial evidence are not converted into universal regimens.
