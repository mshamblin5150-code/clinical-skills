## Conflicts

The age branches are complementary, not contradictory: the USPSTF recommends at least
one screen for every child aged 3 to 5 years, while evidence is insufficient for children
younger than 3 years. Earlier instrument feasibility, source-described screening tests,
and external professional guidance do not convert the younger-than-3 I statement into a
USPSTF recommendation.

`screening-interval` distinguishes the one-screen minimum from the absence of evidence
for an optimal repeat interval. The source-printed American Optometric Association birth,
6-month, 3-year, pre-first-grade, and later 2-year examination schedule is external and
conflicts with treating the USPSTF statement as establishing those additional encounters.
The AAFP positions agree with both USPSTF age branches. The joint professional report
specifies age-dependent assessment modalities but does not replace the USPSTF evidence
conclusion.

Clinical tests and instrument-based tests are alternative or complementary modalities
selected for age and testability; they are not a mandatory bundle. A positive screen has
a source-stated complete-eye-examination referral, but the source establishes no numeric
test-result cutoff. Surgery is acknowledged as treatment context while remaining outside
