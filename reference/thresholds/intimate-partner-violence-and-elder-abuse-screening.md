reporting, interval, and intervention pathways; evidence rationale, accuracy, benefits,

## Conflicts

CONFLICT: ipv-screening-interval — for `asymptomatic-women-reproductive-age`, `no evidence establishes an appropriate screening interval` versus `SOURCE-PRINTED EXTERNAL (WPSI), overlap population: screen at least annually`; the first is the USPSTF evidence boundary and the second is a source-printed external schedule.

CONFLICT: caregiver-abuse-screening-evidence — for `asymptomatic-older-vulnerable-adults`, `evidence insufficient to assess benefits versus harms of screening for caregiver abuse and neglect; I statement` versus `SOURCE-PRINTED EXTERNAL (AMA/AAN): routinely screen all patients for abuse and neglect`; the USPSTF cannot determine net benefit, while the source reports broader external organizations' routine-screening guidance.

The Grade B IPV action applies to asymptomatic women of reproductive age, explicitly
including those who are pregnant and postpartum. The practice-considerations wording
also makes clear that pregnant or postpartum adolescents and adults are included. The
older-or-vulnerable-adult I statement concerns caregiver abuse or neglect and is not a
recommendation for or against screening; it calls for clinical judgment because the
benefit-harm balance cannot be determined. Evidence on IPV screening or interventions
in men and other populations is inadequate and does not extend the Grade B population.

The intervention action is multicomponent and ongoing. Its strongest direct evidence
came from pregnant and postpartum women and was extrapolated to nonpregnant women of
reproductive age. Study durations and session counts are evidence descriptions, not a
mandatory regimen. Brief IPV-only counseling and referral information without a
multicomponent intervention generally did not show benefit. Private, safe questioning
and jurisdiction-specific reporting requirements remain separate safety boundaries.
External organizations' screening schedules and populations retain explicit
source-printed provenance. WPSI's annual interval and AMA/AAN's routine caregiver-abuse
screening are preserved as genuine conflicts rather than replacements for the USPSTF
interval silence or I statement. AAFP separately supports the prior USPSTF guidance.
