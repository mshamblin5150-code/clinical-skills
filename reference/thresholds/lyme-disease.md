## Benefits and harms / implementation boundaries

- Prompt mechanical tick removal and effective repellents reduce exposure; chemical or heat removal can injure skin or provoke tick regurgitation.
- Prophylaxis reduced Lyme disease versus placebo. No serious antibiotic adverse effects were reported, but rashes and gastrointestinal effects occurred. Longer prophylaxis courses add toxicity without demonstrated advantage; topical prophylaxis is unsupported.
- Oral therapy is generally preferred when clinically appropriate because efficacy is equivalent, tolerability is better, cost is lower, and IV access creates potentially serious complications. Drug choice also considers allergy, renal adjustment for beta-lactams, sun-related doxycycline photosensitivity, adherence, cost, and possible anaplasmosis/ehrlichiosis coinfection.
- Macrolides have lower efficacy and are second-line. First-generation cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, pyrazinamide, vancomycin, tigecycline, metronidazole, tinidazole, rifampin, hydroxychloroquine, and fluconazole are not indicated for Lyme disease. Clindamycin, quinine, and atovaquone belong only in recommended babesiosis combinations.
- A mild, self-limited Jarisch-Herxheimer-like intensification may occur within the first 24 hours after antibiotics. Later symptoms should not be labeled this reaction and do not establish microbial burden or prognosis.
- Prolonged retreatment trials did not improve symptoms or quality of life versus placebo. Reported harms included serious allergy, IV catheter complications, ceftriaxone-associated gallbladder pseudolithiasis requiring cholecystectomy, diarrhea, and documented deaths from prolonged IV therapy.
- Positive serology may represent past infection because IgM and IgG can persist for years or decades. Early testing can be falsely negative; low-pretest-probability testing creates false positives. Use validated conventional or modified two-tier serology, not stand-alone assays. Avoid nonstandard serology interpretation, urine antigen, unvalidated DNA tests, lymphocyte transformation tests, and quantitative CD57 assays.
- Seronegative IgG in an untreated patient with months to years of symptoms essentially rules out Lyme disease except laboratory error or rare humoral immunodeficiency. Synovial PCR can remain positive after treatment and does not alone prove active infection.
