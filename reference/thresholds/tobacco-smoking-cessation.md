## Conflicts

CONFLICT: pregnancy-pharmacotherapy | pregnant-tobacco | `evidence is insufficient to determine the balance of benefits and harms of pharmacotherapy` versus `SOURCE-PRINTED EXTERNAL ACOG: consider NRT only after detailed discussion of continued-smoking risks, possible NRT risks, and need for close supervision`; the USPSTF gives an I statement for pharmacotherapy as a class, while ACOG conditionally permits NRT after risk discussion and under close supervision; source pp1 and 12.

The pregnancy pharmacotherapy efficacy and safety rows describe pregnant smokers or
pharmacotherapy-exposed pregnancies and do not inherit the failed-counseling condition.
Only the individualized shared-decision action is limited to pregnant persons for whom
behavioral counseling alone did not work.

CONFLICT: e-cigarette-cessation | adults-pregnant-tobacco | `evidence is insufficient for e-cigarettes; direct patients to other interventions with proven effectiveness and established safety` versus `SOURCE-PRINTED EXTERNAL ACS: do not recommend e-cigarettes as a smoking-cessation method`; the USPSTF gives an I statement plus affirmative redirection, whereas ACS gives a nonrecommendation; source pp1 and 12. ACOG's `SOURCE-PRINTED EXTERNAL ACOG: recommend against e-cigarette use` is a stronger action for the narrower pregnant/postpartum population. AHA's insufficient-evidence counseling conclusion is concordant with the USPSTF uncertainty and is retained under `external-e-cigarette-evidence`, not misclassified as a conflict.

The p11 finding of substantially lower nonnicotine toxicant exposure than combustible
cigarettes is comparative evidence, not a finding that e-cigarettes are risk-free; it
therefore does not conflict with the p7 no-risk-free boundary or resolve the unknown
long-term-harm boundary.
