## Conflicts

The current USPSTF Grade D recommendation applies to the general asymptomatic adult
population and recommends against screening with either neck palpation or ultrasound.
It explicitly does not govern patients with throat symptoms, neck findings, or increased
risk from radiation exposure, family history, or inherited syndromes. The absence of a
screening recommendation for those excluded patients is not a recommendation against
diagnostic evaluation or individualized high-risk care.

The source-printed 1996 USPSTF position also recommended against general-population
screening but used an older Grade C for childhood upper-body radiation exposure, then
defined as insufficient evidence for or against screening. The current statement does
not replace that historical high-risk uncertainty with a new high-risk screening action;
it narrows the current Grade D statement to the general asymptomatic population.

External positions differ in wording and provenance. The source-printed AAFP position
recommended against screening by neck palpation or ultrasound, the American Cancer
Society made no specific screening recommendation, the Canadian checklist omitted a
thyroid examination, and the named thyroid-society nodule guidelines made no
asymptomatic screening recommendation. None is merged into the USPSTF Grade D.

Screening-test accuracy values describe small studies and do not override the screening
recommendation. Likewise, treatment-harm rates and observational immediate-surgery
versus surveillance findings are evidence boundaries, not treatment-selection commands.
The source conditions radioactive iodine on postoperative status, stage, and cancer type
without supplying a universal cutoff.

ADR 0009 disposition:

- Retained the Grade D action, general asymptomatic population, symptomatic and
  neck-finding exclusions, increased-risk exclusion, and the specific radiation,
  family-history, and inherited-syndrome risk factors.
- Retained neck palpation and ultrasound as the studied screening methods, the
  inadequate overall accuracy evidence, and method-specific evidence values without
  turning study cutoffs into screening commands.
- Retained the no-greater-than-small benefit bound, at-least-moderate harm bound,
  negative net benefit, and uncertainty of immediate treatment versus observation.
- Retained surgery as the main treatment, radioactive-iodine selection by postoperative
  status, stage, and cancer type, and the early-stage external-beam-radiation and
  chemotherapy boundary.
- Retained biopsy harms, permanent hypoparathyroidism, recurrent-laryngeal-nerve palsy,
  secondary malignancy, salivary-gland harms, and the source's quantitative harm ranges.
- Retained the diagnosis-without-mortality-change boundary, the source's conclusion that
  overdiagnosis and overtreatment are likely substantial with population-based screening,
  the South Korean screening experience, and latent autopsy-cancer size evidence without
  converting them into clinical mandates.
- Retained the historical USPSTF positions and the source-printed American Cancer
  Society, AAFP, Canadian Task Force, ATA, AACE, ACE, and AME positions with explicit
  external provenance.
- Disease burden, broad survival figures, most study sizes and confidence intervals,
  research requests, publication metadata, disclosures, and reference numerals were not
  interpreted as additional patient actions.
