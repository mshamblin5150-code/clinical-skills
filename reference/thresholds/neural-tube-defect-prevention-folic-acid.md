## Conflicts

No same-population, same-quantity internal conflict was identified. The USPSTF range of
400 to 800 µg daily and the externally reported minimum of 400 µg are compatible.
Prior affected pregnancy and stated very-high-risk or folic-acid-blocking-medication
populations are outside the standard recommendation. The broader list of personal,
partner, or family history, bariatric malabsorption, antiseizure-medicine use, and
folate-related mutations triggers professional discussion but is not automatically an
exclusion. A separate page 5 statement says women at higher risk may require a dose
above the generally available range and should discuss it with a clinician; it gives no replacement dose.
Naturally occurring food folate and folic acid in fortified food are not treated as
substitutes for the recommended supplement.

ADR 0009 disposition:

- retained the daily 400 to 800 µg supplement action, start at least 1 month
  before conception, and continuation through the first 2-3 months of pregnancy;
- the recommendation applies to all persons planning to or who could become pregnant;
  prior affected pregnancy, stated very-high-risk factors, and folic-acid-blocking
  medication populations remain outside the standard action; the broader risk-factor
  list triggers professional consultation, while the possible above-range dose for
  higher-risk women is kept separate and no replacement dose is invented;
- natural food folate, synthetic folic acid in supplements and fortified foods, and the
  finding that fortified-food intake is insufficient for optimal prevention were kept
  distinct; the supplement or multivitamin action remains explicit;
- substantial benefit, reduced neural-tube-defect risk, and lack of serious harms at
  usual doses were retained; study effect estimates, sample sizes, timing comparisons,
  autism findings, twin-delivery comparisons, maternal-cancer findings, and other
  outcome statistics were treated as evidence rather than new action thresholds;
- the Community Preventive Services Task Force campaign and professional/public-health
  dose guidance are explicitly external, as are the ACOG, AAFP, and AAP average-risk
  recommendations; none is merged into the USPSTF recommendation;
- risk associations involving diabetes, obesity, ethnicity, genetics, malabsorption,
  medicines, or family history were not converted into dose algorithms beyond the
  source's consultation boundary;
- embryologic day counts, prevalence, fortification dates, adherence estimates,
  public-comment and research dates, publication metadata, author information,
  disclosures, citation numbers, and reference-list values were not interpreted as
  separate patient-action thresholds.
