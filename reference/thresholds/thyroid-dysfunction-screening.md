## Conflicts

CONFLICT: abnormal-result-confirmation | adults-abnormal-tsh | repeat multiple thyroid-function tests over 3 to 6 months before diagnosis or treatment consideration <> professional groups generally confirm persistent dysfunction over 3 to 6 months unless TSH is greater than 10.0 or less than 0.1 mIU/L | The general repeat-testing interval and the source-described extreme-value exception apply to the same abnormal-result population.

CONFLICT: hypothyroidism-treatment-threshold | subclinical-hypothyroidism | expert opinion generally considers TSH greater than 10.0 mIU/L the treatment-initiation threshold; no clinical-trial threshold proves outcome benefit <> treatment at TSH 4.5 to 10.0 mIU/L is controversial | The source distinguishes an expert-opinion initiation point from a lower range where treatment remains controversial and no clinical-trial threshold proves benefit.

CONFLICT: subclinical-hyperthyroidism-definition | subclinical-hyperthyroidism | TSH below the laboratory lower boundary, usually 0.4 mIU/L, with normal T4 and T3 <> low but detectable TSH about 0.1 to 0.4 mIU/L versus clearly low or undetectable TSH below 0.1 mIU/L | The first value defines the biochemical condition and the second subdivides that same condition by TSH severity; neither is an outcome-based treatment mandate.

CONFLICT: subclinical-hypothyroidism-definition | subclinical-hypothyroidism | elevated TSH above the laboratory upper boundary, commonly but arbitrarily 4.5 mIU/L, with normal T4 <> classify TSH as 4.5 to 10.0 mIU/L or greater than 10.0 mIU/L | The first value defines the biochemical condition and the second subdivides that same condition by TSH severity; the source calls 4.5 mIU/L commonly used but arbitrary.

The USPSTF I statement for asymptomatic nonpregnant adults is neither for nor against
screening. Source-printed ATA/AACE guidance considers screening after age 60 and case
finding in increased-risk persons and women planning pregnancy; source-printed British
guidance recommends against routine screening in healthy adults but favors case finding
in women with nonspecific symptoms. These differ in population, purpose, and organization.

The hyperthyroidism branch generally treats undetectable or below-0.1 mIU/L TSH but
typically does not treat 0.1-to-0.45 mIU/L TSH or thyroiditis.
