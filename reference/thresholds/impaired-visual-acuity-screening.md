## Conflicts

CONFLICT: blindness-definition | older-adult-visual-classification | `best corrected visual acuity 20/200 or worse` versus `best corrected vision less than 20/200 or visual field <=20 degrees`; page 1 includes acuity of exactly 20/200 in blindness while page 2 prints a strict less-than acuity boundary and a separate visual-field criterion, so the discrepancy is preserved rather than normalized.

CONFLICT: screening-interval | external-over-age-65 | `EXTERNAL (AAO): comprehensive ophthalmologist examination every 1-2 years` versus `EXTERNAL (AOA): annual comprehensive eye and vision examination`; these are separately attributed external schedules for the overlapping population older than 65 years and do not resolve the USPSTF I statement.

No USPSTF screening interval or referral threshold is established. The source's risk-factor descriptions do not create an exception to the I statement because it reports no reliable tool for identifying asymptomatic patients who would benefit from screening.

The page 3 broad assessment that dry-AMD antioxidant vitamins and mineral supplements were not associated with increased risk of most serious adverse events is retained alongside the page 4 specific zinc-associated genitourinary hospitalization, antioxidant-associated skin yellowing, and beta-carotene-associated lung-cancer signal in former smokers. These statements are not normalized into a no-harm conclusion.

ADR 0009 disposition:

- retained the I-statement conclusion for asymptomatic adults age 65 or older in primary care; known impairment, care seeking, symptoms, and another medical condition are separated as applicability boundaries, and clinical-judgment evaluation is limited to patients with symptoms;
- retained the source's visual-acuity and blindness definitions, including its internal 20/200 boundary discrepancy, without converting prevalence or driver's-license context into screening thresholds;
- retained current-practice screening modalities and qualitative accuracy limitations; study sensitivities, specificities, sample sizes, prevalence, and screening-outcome estimates were treated as evidence rather than patient-action thresholds;
- retained treatment modalities and source-stated harms after a visual condition has been identified, including specific dry-AMD zinc, antioxidant, and beta-carotene harms; the source's explicit statement that these treatments were not evaluated in screen-detected populations separates treatment efficacy from evidence that screening asymptomatic adults improves outcomes;
- no USPSTF screening interval was inferred. AAO and AOA examination schedules are retained as explicitly `EXTERNAL`, including their conflict in adults older than 65 years, and the AAFP I-statement agreement remains external;
- the p5 referral language is retained only as an evidence gap, not converted into an instruction to refer screen-positive patients; no source-established referral cutoff or follow-up interval was invented;
- diabetic-retinopathy surveillance is outside this recommendation because it is part of diabetes management, and glaucoma is covered by a separate USPSTF recommendation rather than imported here;
- study treatment effects, trial eligibility and follow-up, adverse-event frequencies, research questions, public-comment and publication dates, article metadata, disclosures, citation numbers, and reference-list values were not interpreted as additional actions.
