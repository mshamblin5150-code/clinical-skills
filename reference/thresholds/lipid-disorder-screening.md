## Conflicts

No unresolved same-population, same-quantity conflict was identified. The USPSTF I
statement means that evidence is insufficient to determine the balance of benefits and
harms; it is not a recommendation against screening and does not establish a universal
screening age or interval.

The externally attributed selective and universal screening schedules are affirmative
recommendations from the NHLBI Expert Panel, AAP Bright Futures program, and the
multisociety cholesterol guideline. They differ by risk population: selective screening
may begin at age `2 years` for children with family history or other risk factors, whereas
universal screening applies at ages `9 to 11 years` and again at `17 to 21 years`. The
AAFP statement references the earlier USPSTF I conclusion rather than supplying a
competing screening schedule. These attributed schedules do not fill the USPSTF's absent
age-specific or repeat-interval schedule; under its I statement, the source instead tells
clinicians to use judgment about whether to screen.

The lipid values retained from page 5 are evidence-study definitions and prevalence cut
points. The source states that familial-hypercholesterolemia diagnostic criteria are not
universally accepted and that multifactorial-dyslipidemia cut points are not validated as
cardiovascular-event predictors or made age- and sex-specific. They are therefore not
presented as universal diagnostic or treatment triggers.
