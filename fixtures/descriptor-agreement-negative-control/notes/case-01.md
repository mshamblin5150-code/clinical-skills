## A:

**Differential:**

*Cutaneous abscess of the left foot, plantar great toe* — one-month course with acute escalation; focal swelling of the plantar great toe; exquisite tenderness to light touch; blanched overlying skin with the appearance of a collection beneath the surface; 8/10 pain with inability to bear weight; the documented plan is incision and drainage. **Favored.**

*Residual foreign body of the plantar soft tissue with surrounding inflammatory reaction* — supported by onset at work with a mechanism the patient cannot recall, an indolent one-month course, and the explicit appearance of something below the surface. Frequently coexists with the abscess rather than competing with it, and would be identified at drainage. **Co-favored; drives the plan.**

*Plantar verruca (plantar wart)* — the blanched, thickened plantar appearance is consistent. Argued against by the degree of soft-tissue swelling, the exquisite point tenderness on light touch, and the acute escalation to 8/10 with loss of weight-bearing, none of which a verruca produces. **Less likely.**

*Cellulitis of the left toe* — argued against by the localized, well-circumscribed, blanched appearance without spreading erythema, warmth or proximal streaking, and by the afebrile temperature of 98.4 °F. **Less likely.**

*Acute gouty arthritis of the first metatarsophalangeal joint (podagra)* — the site is classic and 8/10 pain fits. Argued against by the plantar rather than joint-line location, by a one-month indolent course rather than an abrupt monoarticular flare, and by the discrete subcutaneous collection on exam. **Less likely.**

*Osteomyelitis of the distal phalanx* — must be named at one month of unresolved focal foot infection in a 24 pack-year smoker. Argued against today by the afebrile temperature, the intact skin with no sinus tract, and the absence of exposed bone. **Less likely today; becomes the working diagnosis if the wound fails to close after drainage.** Radiograph ordered below on this basis.

*Diabetic foot infection* — considered because an indolent foot lesion in an adult raises undiagnosed diabetes. Argued against by fully preserved and severe pain sensation, which is the opposite of the neuropathic picture. **Less likely; screening recommended below on the strength of BMI 26.5 and age 36.**
