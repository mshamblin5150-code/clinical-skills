## A:

**Differential:**

- **Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis — the documented working diagnosis, and favored here.** Nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, sinus tenderness, headache, pharyngeal erythema, worsening today, with **COVID-19, influenza and group A streptococcus all tested and negative**, which removes the three commonest alternatives by test rather than by argument. **One honest reservation is recorded rather than smoothed away:** the symptoms began *yesterday*. One day does not meet the usual criteria for bacterial sinusitis — 10 days of persistence, or severe symptoms with high fever for 3 to 4 days, or double worsening after initial improvement. The diagnosis is the clinician's and it is carried as given; the duration argues that a viral prodrome is at least as likely, and that is stated.
- **Acute coronary syndrome or other cardiac cause of the chest pain — cannot be excluded, and was not evaluated.** This is the most consequential entry in the list. A **48-year-old man with documented diabetes mellitus** reports **sharp chest pain**, generalized weakness graded 8/10, and has a **magnesium of 1.6**. Diabetes is an atherosclerotic risk equivalent and is the classic reason for atypical or blunted ischemic presentation. **No ECG, no troponin, no chest x-ray and no chest wall palpation are documented.** The cardiac exam recorded is S1, S2 and 2+ equal pulses — a normal exam does not exclude ischemia. Arguments against: the pain is described as sharp rather than pressure-like, there is no documented radiation, diaphoresis, exertional trigger or dyspnea, and there is a plausible competing explanation in the acute febrile illness. **None of those arguments is a test, and the difference matters.** Worked up in the Plan.
- **Viral upper respiratory infection with myalgia — plausible and not excluded.** Abrupt onset yesterday, generalized body aches, sore throat, cough, congestion. Against it: influenza and SARS-CoV-2 are both negative; but negative rapid antigen testing does not exclude either organism, nor the many respiratory viruses not tested for.
- **Musculoskeletal or costochondral chest wall pain from coughing — plausible.** Sharp quality, a new cough, and diffuse myalgia all fit. **It cannot be asserted as the explanation, because no chest wall palpation is documented** — reproducibility on palpation is the finding that would place it, and it was not obtained.
- **Pneumonia or lower respiratory tract infection — less likely.** Supported by cough, chest pain and **breath sounds diminished in all fields**. Against it: the diminution is diffuse rather than focal, no crackles, rhonchi or dullness are documented, oxygen saturation is 96% on room air, respiratory rate is 18, and there is no documented fever above 100.4 °F. No chest radiograph was obtained, so it is not excluded either.
- **Hypomagnesemia as a contributor to the weakness — supported by a given.** Magnesium 1.6 mg/dL. Low magnesium produces weakness, muscle cramping, and — the reason it belongs in this particular note — **QT prolongation and arrhythmia risk in a patient who is simultaneously reporting undifferentiated chest pain.** Two documented givens plausibly explain the level: **diabetes** causes renal magnesium wasting, and a **prior colon resection** impairs enteral magnesium absorption. Both are already in the history and neither is invented.
- **Streptococcal pharyngitis — excluded by test.** Rapid strep negative.
- **Influenza — excluded by test, with the sensitivity caveat above.** Rapid influenza negative.
- **COVID-19 — excluded by test, with the same caveat.** Negative, and no sick contacts documented.
- **Pulmonary embolism — less likely.** Sharp chest pain and weakness would raise it. Against: no dyspnea, no hypoxia (96% on room air), no tachycardia beyond 96, no unilateral leg swelling or calf tenderness, no documented immobilization, malignancy, surgery within the window, or prior thromboembolism. Pretest probability is low and no imaging is proposed on this presentation alone.
- **Peritonsillar or deep neck space infection — excluded.** No trismus, no uvular deviation, no muffled voice, no unilateral bulge.

**"Labs good" against magnesium 1.6 — the two givens contradict each other, and the note does not pick the comfortable one.** The source records the laboratory studies as good and then records a magnesium of 1.6 mg/dL, which is below range. Both are given and both are carried. **The clinician acted on the low value** — an over-the-counter magnesium supplement is in the plan — so the value was seen; what is wrong is the summary characterizing it as normal. The level is treated here as the abnormal it is, with a documented cause available in the history and a recheck scheduled.

**Sharp chest pain — addressed, not filed.** Named as its own problem above and carried into the Plan as an ECG, a troponin and explicit emergency return instructions. It is coded as **R07.89, other chest pain**, which is the honest code for a symptom that was documented and not characterized. It is not folded into the sinusitis and it is not attributed to the cough, because nothing in the record supports either attribution over ischemia.

**Diminished breath sounds in all fields — addressed, not filed.** Diffuse rather than focal, with no wheeze, crackle or hypoxia. **Named conflicts between the givens — each one stated, none dissolved:**
1. **Two corticosteroids against documented diabetes mellitus.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IM plus a 6-day oral dose pack will raise his blood glucose materially over the next week. Counseled, with home monitoring three to four times daily and a call threshold. Neither steroid is dropped — the acute indication stands and the conflict is managed rather than made to disappear.
2. **Albuterol against a documented magnesium of 1.6.** Beta-2 agonists shift magnesium and potassium intracellularly and will push an already-low magnesium lower. In a patient who is simultaneously reporting undifferentiated chest pain, that is an arrhythmia-relevant combination and not a footnote. Counseled to use the inhaler as needed rather than scheduled, magnesium replacement started today, and the level rechecked with a potassium and a calcium.
3. **Ceftriaxone 1 g plus amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg — overlapping beta-lactam coverage for the same infection.** An IM loading dose followed by an oral course is a defensible walk-in strategy, but the overlap is stated rather than left implicit, and the oral course carries the cumulative diarrhea and *C. difficile* risk of both.
4. **Corticosteroids against a documented history of diverticulitis and colon resection.** Systemic steroids raise the risk of diverticular complication and, more importantly, **blunt the peritoneal signs that would announce one**. - Magnesium supplement — the commonest side effect is loose stool; separate it from other medication by 2 hours.
- Saline irrigation technique and the distilled-water rule.
- Acetaminophen ceiling of 3 g in 24 hours, and the fact that many combination cold products already contain it. **NSAIDs are to be avoided while on the steroid, given his colon history.**
