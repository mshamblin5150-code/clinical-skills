## A:

Differential:

**Scabies** — the clinician's documented working diagnosis, and a given. Supported by generalized
itching, widespread excoriations, a one-week course that has not resolved, and an animal contact.
**Favored**, because it is stated. What would ordinarily place it — burrows, finger web spaces,
flexor wrists, axillae, periumbilical or genital lesions — was not documented, so the diagnosis
rests on the impression and not on a recorded distribution. Confirmation by skin scraping or
dermoscopy is proposed rather than assumed.

**Canine sarcoptic mange transmitted from the dog** — she names the dog herself and the dog is in
the home. Zoonotic *Sarcoptes scabiei* var. *canis* produces intense itching and excoriation in
humans at contact sites, does not burrow or complete its cycle in human skin, and is self-limited
once the animal is treated. This changes the plan rather than the medication: the dog needs a
veterinarian regardless of which of these two is right. Reasonable second.

**Delusional infestation (Ekbom syndrome) or drug-associated formication** — a crawling sensation
localized to the ears, self-inflicted excoriations, an admitted habit of digging in the canals with
fingernails, a documented substance use disorder, and failure to resolve one week after a visit for
the same complaint. This is named because it is the diagnosis that permethrin does not treat and
that a second course of permethrin would delay. Less likely on the record as it stands, and not
excludable on it either. **PROPOSED reasoning — verify before use.**

**Asteatotic (xerotic) eczema** — xerosis is documented on both ankles, this patient is 57, and
xerotic itch is generalized, worse in dry conditions and produces exactly this pattern of
excoriation with no primary lesion. Emollients are in the plan on this basis whatever else is true.
Less likely as the whole explanation, since it does not account for the ear canal sensation.

**Pruritus secondary to a systemic cause** — cholestasis, chronic kidney disease, iron deficiency,
thyroid disease, polycythemia or lymphoma. Nothing in this encounter argues for any of them and
nothing excludes them; no laboratory studies were obtained. Less likely, and the reason a lab panel
is proposed if itching persists after treatment.

**Eczematous otitis externa from repeated digital trauma** — scratch marks in both canals with
itching and no discharge, no canal edema and no tragal tenderness. Less likely as an infection,
likely as a mechanical/eczematous contributor, and it is the reason the ear canals get an emollient
and a stop-picking instruction rather than an antibiotic drop.

**Allergic contact dermatitis** — no new product, detergent, jewelry or topical exposure is
documented. Less likely.

Preexisting diagnoses (ICD10): Essential (primary) hypertension **I10**; nicotine dependence,
cigarettes, uncomplicated **F17.210**; other psychoactive substance dependence, uncomplicated
**F19.20** — *proposed with reservation: the source records `sud` with no substance and no status,
so the substance, the severity and whether it is current or remote are all undocumented; confirm
before this code is entered*; acquired absence of both cervix and uterus (status post total
hysterectomy) **Z90.710**; acquired absence of other specified parts of digestive tract
(cholecystectomy, appendectomy) **Z90.49**.

Final diagnosis: **Scabies — B86.** With: pruritus, unspecified **L29.9**; xerosis cutis **L85.3**;
essential hypertension **I10**, uncontrolled at today's reading; bradycardia, unspecified **R00.1**;
nicotine dependence, cigarettes, uncomplicated **F17.210**; body mass index 20.0–20.9, adult
**Z68.20**.

**The two vitals are part of this assessment, not a header on it.** BP 147/81 in a patient carrying
a hypertension diagnosis is an uncontrolled reading and is treated as one below. It is **not**
explained away by the inferred lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide — an inferred regimen does not
settle a question the givens raise, and no medication reconciliation was done today. HR 59 is below
the normal range. It may be physiologic, and the single most likely non-physiologic cause is a rate
controlling agent she may be taking and that nobody has written down; that is a reconciliation task
and an ECG, not a diagnosis.

Age-appropriate screening to consider: mammography (biennial or annual, age 57). Colorectal cancer
screening — due, ages 45–75. Cervical cancer screening — status post total hysterectomy; screening
may be discontinued if the hysterectomy was for benign disease and there is no history of CIN 2+,
and **the indication for the hysterectomy is not documented**, so confirm before stopping.
Osteoporosis — DEXA, ordinarily 65, considered earlier here because current smoking is a risk
factor. Lipid panel and HbA1c. Hepatitis C and HIV one-time screening. Depression screening (PHQ-9)
and substance use screening (AUDIT-C plus a substance-specific tool), the latter directly relevant
to the differential above. **Low-dose CT lung cancer screening — she is 57 and smokes 1 pack per
day, so she meets the age criterion and needs 20 pack-years to qualify; the source records no
smoking duration, so pack-years cannot be computed. Ask the duration and order the LDCT if 20 is
met.** Immunizations: influenza, pneumococcal (indicated at any adult age for a current smoker),
