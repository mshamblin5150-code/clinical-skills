## A:

**Differential:**

- **Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis with concurrent bilateral acute otitis media — favored.** Sinus pressure and congestion with facial pain that is *worse today* rather than improving, rhinorrhea, sneezing, cough, head achy, pharyngeal erythema, and — decisively — **bilaterally bulging tympanic membranes**, which is a middle-ear effusion under pressure and not a finding of an uncomplicated viral cold. The clinician's own plan (amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg plus systemic corticosteroid) treats this entity. The short 1-to-2-day duration is the argument against it and is stated rather than smoothed over; the worsening trajectory and the ear findings are what carry it.
- **Acute infective otitis externa, right ear — favored, concurrent.** **Right external auditory canal swelling and erythema**, with earache. Directly treated by the neomycin-polymyxin B-hydrocortisone otic drops in the plan. Not an alternative to the above — a second, simultaneous diagnosis.
- **Acute viral upper respiratory infection, including COVID-19 — less likely as the whole explanation, but not excluded.** Supported by the 1-to-2-day onset, sneezing, sore throat, rhinorrhea, and a **COVID-positive daughter-in-law**. Argued against as a complete account by the bulging tympanic membranes and the swollen, erythematous ear canal, neither of which a viral URI produces. **Nothing excludes it, because no test was performed** — this remains open and is coded as an exposure.
- **Streptococcal pharyngitis — less likely.** Sore throat and pharyngeal erythema are present. Against it: **no cervical lymphadenopathy on exam** (explicitly documented as absent), no tonsillar exudate, no fever documented, and the presence of cough, sneezing and rhinorrhea — three findings that argue away from group A strep. Centor score is low. No rapid strep was done.
