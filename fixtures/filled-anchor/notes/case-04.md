## A:

Differential:

**Acute febrile viral syndrome — COVID-19, influenza or another respiratory virus** — 5 days of
fever, headache, myalgia, sore throat with pharyngeal erythema, productive cough, diarrhea, and a
household contact with the same illness. Bilateral bulging tympanic membranes are a recognized
complication of exactly this. **Favored.** The COVID test is already ordered and is a given;
**influenza and RSV PCR are added, because influenza is the one with a treatment window and he is
not up to date with vaccinations.**

**Acute otitis media, bilateral, left with erythema** — a documented exam finding, not a
speculation. Bulging is the cardinal sign, it is bilateral, and he is on day 5 of an illness that
is not improving. Treated below.

**Community-acquired pneumonia** — productive cough, pleuritic pain, chest tightness, 5 days of
fever and **51 pack-years**. Against: afebrile today, SpO2 97%, RR 18 and a normal lung exam. The
lung exam is filled, not given, so it is the weakest of those four points. **A chest radiograph is
the discriminator, is not in the source plan, and is ordered here.**

**Recurrent spontaneous pneumothorax** — **he has a documented punctured lung**, he is a heavy
smoker, and he presents with chest tightness and pain worse on deep inspiration. Against: SpO2 97%,
no distress, no tachycardia, symmetric breath sounds. Less likely, and it is the second reason the
chest film is not optional. This is the history item most easily lost between the `hx:` line and
the plan, and it is named here for that reason.

**Acute pyelonephritis or urinary tract infection** — **right costovertebral angle tenderness with
5 days of fever.** Against: afebrile now, no dysuria, frequency or hematuria documented, and the
abdominal tenderness is diffuse rather than localized. Not excludable on any of that.
**Urinalysis with microscopy and a urine culture are mandatory and are not named in the source
plan.** This is the highest-consequence item in the encounter — a right CVA tenderness that reaches
the exam and stops there is the defect this note exists to catch.

**Nephrolithiasis** — right CVA tenderness. Against: the pain is not described as colicky and no
hematuria is documented. The urinalysis covers both this and the entry above.

**Acute infectious gastroenteritis** — 5 days of diarrhea, diffuse lower abdominal tenderness, a
symptomatic household contact, and a viral syndrome that commonly includes it. Reasonable second,
and part of the same illness rather than a separate one.

**Bile-acid diarrhea after cholecystectomy** — the source says the diarrhea is worse **specifically
after he eats**, and he is status post cholecystectomy. That combination is the classic
presentation and it is a chronic mechanism that an acute illness can unmask. Less likely as the
whole 5-day picture, and worth naming because it is the reading of "especially after he eats" that
survives a negative infectious workup.

**GERD flare or peptic ulcer disease** — documented GERD with epigastric tenderness today.
Against: the epigastric pain is new, febrile and accompanied by diarrhea. Less likely alone;
*H. pylori* testing is proposed if the epigastric pain outlasts the illness.

**Acute pericarditis or myocarditis** — a viral prodrome with pleuritic chest tightness. This is
what makes the clinician's ECG and troponin the right orders rather than defensive ones. Against:
no friction rub, no positional relief described, HR 96 and BP 105/72 stable. Less likely; the
ordered studies address it.

**Acute coronary syndrome** — chest tightness 5/10 in a 45-year-old with 51 pack-years. Against:
the pain is reproducibly pleuritic, it sits inside a 5-day febrile illness, and there is no
exertional pattern, radiation or diaphoresis. Less likely — and the ECG and troponin are already
given, so the differential and the plan agree.

**Pulmonary embolism** — pleuritic pain and chest tightness. Against: SpO2 97%, HR 96, no unilateral
leg swelling or calf tenderness, no immobilization, surgery or malignancy documented. Low
probability; PERC applied rather than D-dimer sent reflexively, and revisited if the chest film is
clean and the pain persists.

**Streptococcal pharyngitis** — sore throat with pharyngeal erythema. Against: **no cervical
lymphadenopathy — a given** — no tonsillar exudate, afebrile today, and a prominent cough. Centor
0–1. Less likely; no rapid strep indicated.
